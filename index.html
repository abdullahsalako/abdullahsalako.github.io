A video editor who reads the data as well as the footage.

I’m Aderemi Abdullah Salako, a video editor and content strategist based in Abuja, Nigeria. I’m a self-taught DaVinci Resolve editor with experience creating YouTube and social-media content, and I work with creators and brands remotely.

I come to editing with a data-analyst’s habit of asking what the audience actually did, and a creative technologist’s curiosity about the tools. The goal is always the same: a cut that feels effortless to watch and clear in what it’s saying.

My approach

Every edit starts with one question: what should the viewer feel or do by the end? I structure footage around that answer, cutting what doesn’t earn its place and giving the moments that matter room to land.

Rhythm is what makes a video feel alive. I shape the speed of each scene so energy rises and falls on purpose, tighter for short-form hooks, more breathing room for long-form storytelling.

Audiences forgive soft focus before they forgive bad audio. I clean dialogue, sync tracks and balance music and effects so the sound supports the story instead of competing with it.

Colour carries mood. I correct and match shots first, protect skin tones, then build a look that fits the story, restrained where it should be and bold only where it counts.

Hooks, structure, captions and format are decisions about attention. I design each edit for the platform it will live on, and treat retention data as feedback for the next cut.

Red Eye Effect: restraint, then one red detail.

How a quiet close-up becomes an unsettling reveal when colour does the talking instead of effects.

Frame data

The red eye is about 0.001% of the frame.

I measured the final graded frame. Roughly half its pixels are neutral, a third are warm skin and beard tones, and a tenth is the green wall behind. The red accent is a handful of pixels, and it is the detail the entire grade is built around.

OverviewGoal & challengeProcessBefore / afterToolsResult

01 Project overview

Red Eye Effect is a 31-second cinematic colour study. It takes a quiet, intimate close-up and shifts it, through grading alone, into something quietly strange. The piece is a deliberate exercise in using colour as the storytelling device.

02 Goal & creative challenge

Goal. Build tension without turning the frame into an effect. The image should feel intimate first, then quietly strange, a look that rewards a second viewing.

Challenge. Keep the surrounding palette restrained and the skin tone natural, while still letting a single saturated detail carry the emotional shift.

03 Editing & grading workflow

The treatment keeps the palette restrained and contrast controlled, reserving saturation for the eye reveal. Each adjustment was chosen to protect the skin tone and let the emotional shift arrive through colour rather than noise.

  1. 1Intent

    Define the look

    Decide what the grade should feel like before touching a control.

  2. 2Foundation

    Balance the shot

    Neutral exposure and white balance so every later decision is predictable.

  3. 3Protect

    Hold the skin tone

    Keep the face believable while the surrounding palette stays restrained.

  4. 4Accent

    Selective colour

    Reserve saturation for the eye so the reveal arrives through colour alone.

  5. 5Deliver

    Review & export

    Check the grade across the whole 31 seconds, then render the final film.

Principles behind the grade
  • Restraint first: contrast controlled, palette muted.
  • One accent only. Saturation is spent where the story needs it.
  • Skin tone is never sacrificed for the look.
  • Colour, not noise, carries the emotional shift.
